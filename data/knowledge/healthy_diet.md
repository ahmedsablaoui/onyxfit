# Healthy Diet: Evidence-Based Basics

## Core principles

The World Health Organization describes four principles of a healthy diet:
- Adequacy: provide sufficient nutrients without excess.
- Balance: balance energy intake and expenditure and include appropriate sources of carbohydrates, fats, and protein.
- Moderation: limit nutrients and ingredients that can negatively affect health.
- Diversity: eat a variety of nutritious foods across food groups.

## Food variety

A varied diet can include:
- Vegetables and fruits
- Legumes such as beans, lentils, and chickpeas
- Whole grains
- Nuts and seeds
- Appropriate protein sources, including eggs, fish, dairy, or other foods suited to the person's dietary preferences

## Food quality

Prefer a variety of minimally processed foods. Limit excessive free sugars, salt, and unhealthy fats. Food safety matters too.

## Individual differences

Dietary needs and suitable food choices can vary with age, activity, health conditions, culture, preferences, and the foods a person can access and afford.

This article provides general education. It is not a personalized diet prescription or a substitute for professional medical nutrition advice.
